# Dragonspire Castle (castle v4, the great keep) as built

The castle stands on the home island's crown and was rebuilt in six stages to the approved plan in [blueprints/castle-v4/](blueprints/castle-v4/README.md) (PRs #78, #81, #82, #83, #85, #86), then grown 1.3× round the hero (Mitchell's pick B, October 3: see "The castle grown 1.3×" below). This file describes what is built now; the plan's pictures and `design.json` are the intent, and the departures from them are listed under "Known departures". Earlier versions (v1 to v3: the donjon, the pavilions, the ward gates, the postern, the belvedere, the service ward, the ramp) are gone and appear only as history in [DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](DESIGN_DECISIONS.md).

Coordinates are island cells (1 cell = 1 m, north is negative Z); a rectangle `[x0, z0, x1, z1]` is in cell edges. The crown (the bailey's turf) is world height 11 (`CROWN_Y`); the terrace is 2.5 m over it. The main axis is x = 86 and the cross axis z = 92. Everything is stated once, in `src/world/castle/plan.ts`; the doors, partitions, stairs and furniture footprints that draw a building also drive its collision.

## The idea

One composition on the axis, a mirror image either side of it. From the south: the ledge road, the bridge over the moat, the gatehouse, the avenue and the dragon fountain between four lawn panels, the cour, the grand stair, the terrace, the great keep with its great door, and behind it the moat's basin and the rock.

- **The great keep** is the tallest thing by far: 32 m square (x 70..102, z 20..52), battlements 36 m over the crown, four corner turrets to blue spires at 57 m. Its front half stands on the terrace, its back half out in the moat's basin on a battered plinth stepped down to the bed. The play camera shows its base, the stair and the great door; its full height shows on the approach, in the overview and on the title screen.
- **Mirrored about the axis:** the north range (kitchen / great hall / keep / chapel / solar), the stables and the barracks, the two walled gardens, the paddock and the training yard, the parterre's four panels, the two garden stairs, the towers, the lamps.
- **Every walk joins another:** the paving is one connected network, and every yard, garden and lawn can be walked into from the gate. Tests hold it (`tests/keep.test.ts`).

## The terrace and the grand stair

The upper court runs from the north curtain to a retaining wall at z 58, a podium standing out on the axis to z 63. Its edge is a retaining wall carrying a parapet 1.5 m over the paving, open only where the three stairs come down: the grand stair on the axis (16 m wide, thirteen steps of 19 cm between cheek walls, a champion in an open-faced plumed helm on each cheek) and the two garden stairs (6 m wide, thirteen steps each, on the gardens' own axes). The ground under every flight is graded just under its treads (tested).

## The outer works

- **Curtain:** one rectangle on centre lines x 29..143, z 28..131 (114 by 103 m), 2.2 thick, 9 m high all round, the wall walk at +9 between crenellated parapets. The north face is two runs that meet the keep's flanks square; the north range's back walls stand against the curtain's inner face under its walk. A dressed outer bank with a stone coping holds the moat; the curtain, the towers and the gate's drums rise out of the water on battered plinths founded on the bed.
- **Towers:** twelve round towers, evenly spaced and mirrored: a corner tower at each corner (a stage taller, a blue-slate spire, a pennant), three wall towers on each of the west and east faces and two on the south either side of the gate, standing out into the moat. The corner towers are 4.9 m in radius and 15.5 m to the walk's storey, the wall towers 4.2 m and 14.5 m, standing 2.6 m out from the wall's line so the walls die into their drums. Round parts are laid in rings of flat stones. Every tower opens onto the wall walk at a doorway; the walk itself is not playable yet.
- **Gatehouse:** on the axis in the south run (86, 131), two flat-topped drums 17 m high with flags either side of a passage six wide under the ceremonial arch (a ring of dressed voussoirs on one radius and a keystone with the lord's shield). As in v3 apart from the drums' tops and its size, so the keep's spires rise over it seen from the farm.
- **The moat:** rings all four sides, its still water 2.5 m under the turf and its bed 5.5 m under it and mirroring the walls round it. It widens into a basin behind the keep (the keep's back rises straight out of the water) and runs round both sides to the gate. Two springs spill into the basin from arched spouts in the north bank; a sluice in the west bank feeds the west fall off the brink through a rock channel; the moat leaves under the gate terrace by the bastion's culvert as the south fall into a pool, and a stream runs on from it. The rim of natural rock beyond the north bank is not walkable.

## The approach

From the portal court the **climb** takes the rock's south-east corner in four straight flights 5 m wide (15 risers of 18.3 cm and 38 cm treads to a flight, the hero's easy pace; nosed slabs over darker risers) with flagged landings between them, two north and two west, walled on both sides (raking copings, a pier at every change of pitch, buttresses on the tall south wall, lanterns at the foot and the head), to the **landing**, a walled terrace at the stair's head. From it the **ledge road** runs west level with the crown between its parapets, a grass verge to its south and the moat's bank to its north, to the **gate terrace** on its battered **bastion** (string course, cornice, the culvert's round arch in its south face). The **bridge** crosses the moat to the gate on two round arches either side of a pier with pointed cutwaters; its deck is walked at the crown while the water runs under the arches (`ZoneLayout.decks`, `WorldView.floorAt`). The ledge walk carries on west past lanterns to the **lookout**, a walled terrace on a natural knoll; a stone bench looks out over the parapet at the lookout and another at the landing. Parapets follow the rock's brink in straight runs with turf between them and the paved way, ending in capped piers.

## The bailey

Lawn everywhere a walk, court or yard does not pave it; there are no barrels, crates or carts lying about the lawns.

- **Avenue:** six wide, paved, from the gate through the forecourt (clipped yews and a pair of lamps either side) and the fountain plaza to the cour, with paired lamp posts, banded at the gate's threshold.
- **Great parterre:** four lawn panels round the round fountain plaza, mirrored about both axes, each edged in low clipped box, its plaza side cut back in a concave arc, a lozenge bed of flowers in the middle, spiral and tiered topiary, and blossom trees (the island's grown trees, pink toward the keep and white toward the gate), and a bench on each diagonal facing the fountain.
- **Dragon fountain:** the centrepiece at the crossing of the axes, facing the gate: a round basin with a moulded kerb, an octagonal pedestal of cream ashlar, and the bronze dragon (Mitchell's pick A), a chunky blocky sentinel sitting upright like a guardian lion, about 12 high (basin 14.6 m across), its forelegs jointed (shoulder, elbow, forearm, paw).
- **Cour d'honneur:** a paved court below the grand stair, the garden walks entering at its sides, a clipped yew either side of each.
- **Kerbs and walks:** wherever lawn or gravel meets the paving a dressed stone kerb edges it; the walks are the avenue, the cross walk, and the walks round the parterre. The paving is one laid pattern of flagstones everywhere.
- **Kitchen garden (west):** four boxed vegetable quarters with fruit trained on its walls round a well, bee skeps at the end of its cross walk. **Privy garden (east):** lawns with shade maples, benches, flower beds, a basin and a rose arbour against the curtain. Each has a gate into its yard and one on its cross walk out to the cour.
- **Yards:** the stable yard (trough against the stable wall, mounting block, cart, barrels) and the muster yard (well, arms racks), with lamps either side of the stables' and barracks' doors.
- **Paddock (west):** fenced, its gate on the south walk, an oak, a hay rack, a water trough and five horses. **Training yard (east):** archery butts before the barracks with the shooting line along the lane from the gate, two rows of pells, arms racks.

## Stairs and floors

A stair is a flight, or flights with half landings, inside a room (`stairs` on a building; `stairProblems` checks the riser rules). On the ground floor its foot row takes you up; upstairs its head row takes you down; the rest of the flight and the stairwell are solid. You arrive beside it on the other floor, outside the trigger. A short fade switches the player's height, collision grid, camera and pet. Rooms open to a ceiling over the next floor are `voids` on the upper floor: no floor there, and the room below stays in view from the galleries. Daises are raised floors.

## The castle rock

The crown stands on a mountain of natural rock, and every cliff on the island (and in the other zones' outdoor rock) is built the same way:

- **Beds:** the rock is cut on natural bedding planes at irregular spacing (mostly tall beds of two to six, now and then a thin one) that dip and roll across the land by up to about 20 degrees, so ledges run at different heights along a face and no two cliffs step alike. The mine's cave walls keep their even, stacked strata.
- **Weathered faces:** above the foot every face is pushed back into the rock by different amounts along the cliff and up it, standing out as buttresses, falling back into bays, leaning out over the bed below or cut back under the one above. The foot stays on its cell edge and the rock at the island's edge stays put over the underside.
- **The look (Mitchell's pick, style C, October 2):** dark blue-grey rock heavily softened by moss, rounded weathered shoulders and big irregular masses of different sizes, pines and ferns on the ledges and the lip, waterfalls with mist at their foot. Never rows of same-size columns, ribs, terrace rings or battlement-like caps.
- **Paint:** big upright fractured facets, each catching the light from its own side, long vertical joints, a few broken bedding lines and the odd patch of fractures; dark rain streaks run down the faces and ochre and cool stains drift across them; on the keep's lush rock moss spreads over most tops and down the rounded shoulders, elsewhere only in broad patches on the flattest ledges, never as a line along every bed.
- **Rock masses, boulders and scree:** a kit of ten weathered rock masses (rounded, faceted lumps, each its own profile: low domes, tall rounded blocks, slab-topped ledges, leaning knuckles) stands out of every face taller than a metre, overlapped at very different sizes and turns: a few huge shoulders, many middling lumps, small knuckles slumped at their feet, and on the tallest faces more masses bedded higher up, so the whole height breaks up. Each is fitted to the ground: shrunk until it never stands out over a road, paving or a lawn at the foot, lowered until it never rises through the ground on top. Moss drapes over every crown in a soft, lumpy sheet. Boulders sized to the face lie fallen at its foot and scree spills a little way out over the grass. None crowds a fall.
- **Plants:** grass grows wherever the rock lies flat, on the ledges and along the top lip; on the masses' front shoulders the odd pine, ferns (arching fronds set with pairs of blocky leaflets shortening to the tip) and moss cushions; small trees only in a few planted pockets, bare rock between (never a row along every ledge).
- **The road's edge:** the ledge's parapets stand on the rock's lip, no built footing between road and rock; the masses' tops follow a broad noise along the face, some rounding off right under the lip, others stopping well short. Cliff lips sit just above the turf, and the lawn rolls over every lip in a fringe of leaning tufts.
- **Masses:** on the lush keep the faces are only lightly cut back, so the rock masses shape them; even faces a storey or less high take a broken rim of masses, so no grass plateau ends in a bare cut. Every face carries one big weathering gradient, warmer and darker at its damp foot and cooler and lighter toward the crown, and moss lies only in broad patches on faces that truly face up (laid from above, never tracing a bed). On the west a buttress of the crown runs out to the island's edge, breaking the face's tiers; on the east the shoulder runs close under the crown so the two cliffs merge into one broken massif. The island's hanging underside is the same rock masses turned upside down, of very different depths, none under a fall.
- **Spurs:** at irregular intervals along the foot of every tall face a spur of the same rock runs out over the ground below, high where it leaves the face and stepping down to its nose.
- **The island's side:** a sheer skirt of the same rock drops from the very edge of the land (ground or cliff top) to below the underside, so the island is one closed mass with no sky showing through; the underside's hanging columns and core are the cliff's darker rock. Slivers of land sticking out into the void break off. No rock floats beside the castle: the drifting islets are two loose clusters far out off the south-west, well below the land (never in the castle's silhouette from any camera), each an inverted cone of the cliff rock under a domed grass cap with tufts over its rim and roots hanging under it, bobbing slowly. The castle rock wraps all four sides: on the north, east and west the crown's natural edge wanders in bays and spurs beyond the moat's outer bank and drops to the shoulder. Ledges partway down a face are bare rock (turf only on the top), cut by gullies into separate buttresses. Roads and terraces over the rock are walled by the parapet, which ends in capped piers; the climb from the portal court is four flights of real steps between walls (see "The approach").

## Grass, water and sky

The Veil is lit at the golden hour: a warm, low sun, a lilac sky warming to peach at the horizon, a sea of soft cloud below the island's edge, a bright sky bounce so shade stays readable. No stars.


Every grassy cell on the island grows a continuous shell lawn: clipped short with mower stripes inside the castle, taller in the meadows, where the blades are slender (two interleaved fields of them keep the sward full). The meadow at the rock's foot rolls gently, easing to level ground round the plots, paths, rock and water. The field plots each grow one crop in three rows on ridged soil (cabbages, lettuces, carrots, leeks or red cabbages, turning through the grid). The moat's still water mirrors the walls round it (a lazy, layer-limited planar reflection, about 4 ms at the bridge). Still water in the fountain basin and the waterfall's step pools shares one pool water: a depth tint from pale at the rim to deep at the heart, drifting ripples, rings and broken foam where water lands, the zone's sky reflected toward grazing angles (calmed, so it reads as sky, not blotches) and soft sun glints on the ripples. The stream, spring and pond use the terrain's water, which has the same ripples, sky reflection, glints and a lace of foam at the shore. Falling water (the dragon's jaws, the jets, the waterfall) pours in long streaks, white with air toward its foot.

## How it is built

- **Plan:** `src/world/castle/plan.ts` states every number (curtain, twelve towers, keep, terrace, stairs, range, zones, walks, moat, bridge, approach, climb, crown outline, water, farm). `src/data/zoneMaps.ts` (`buildKeep`) runs the castle modules in turn and lists `KEEP_BUILDINGS`.
- **Layout, one module per concern** (`src/world/castle/`): `site.ts` (the shared placing kit: cells, levels, walls, props), `ground.ts` (the crown's natural edge, the moat, the water below the rock), `approach.ts` (the climb, the ledge, the gate terrace, the bridge), `bailey.ts` (terrace, stairs, walks, lawns, fountain, gardens, yards), `curtain.ts` (wall runs, towers, gatehouse).
- **Buildings:** `keepSpec.ts` and `keepModel.ts` (the great keep: spec, then its own model), `rangeSpecs.ts` (the other six buildings, each with its `CastleLook`), `rangeParts.ts` (the slate roofs, lanterns and flèche), and `buildingModel.ts` (the shared building builder that reads `CastleLook`: windows, roof, buttresses, porch, fills against the curtain, galleries, ceilings, lofts).
- **Props** (`src/world/castleProps/`: `curtain.ts`, `water.ts`, `approach.ts`, `bailey.ts`): the model builders for the castle's own prop kinds, merged lazily into the prop registry in `src/world/props.ts`. Shared measures are in `src/data/castle.ts`.
- **Checks:** `tests/castle-geometry.test.ts` is the geometry audit (the castle is built as the game builds it, every prop and building broken back into blocks, and nothing may clip, float or crowd a gate; the moat's water level, banks above the water and every drum on a plinth are tested too); `tests/keep.test.ts`, `castle-floors.test.ts`, `keep-model.test.ts` and `range.test.ts` hold the layout, floor travel, the keep and the north range.
- **Looking:** `npm run inspect -- bailey` captures the castle (the rock from below, the approach and moat, the keep, the range and its roofs, the fountain, the gardens and yards through the gameplay camera, the wall walks, the island beyond; `bailey:rock` for the rock alone, `bailey:<view>+<view>` for named views, `bailey-angles:<view>` for orbits); `npm run inspect -- castle` captures the plans and every room.

## The castle grown 1.3×

Mitchell picked growing the castle 1.3× (pick B, October 3) because the hero (2.1 m in his helm) made a castle built to real-life sizes look small. Everything castle-built grew 1.3× about the castle's middle and was set out again on the cell grid; what did not grow:

- **The stones.** The block grid is fixed in metres (courses 0.5 m, stones 1.0 m), so there are simply more courses: every building's length and depth is whole metres, its walls 0.9 m thick, every wall height, sill, head, parapet and floor line on half metres.
- **The crown and the rock's height.** The crown stays 11 m over the court, so the climb is no taller: the rock's footprint grew to carry the castle, the walls and the keep grew over it.
- **Creatures.** Horses (in the paddock and in their stalls) and people keep their own size.

The stairs follow a hero's house rule (`STAIR_RULE`: risers of 18 to 20 cm, at most 17 to a flight, flights at least 2 wide), the castle's doors are 3 wide with their leaves 4.9 m to the point (the great door 6 wide), parapets and rails stand at the hero's chest (1.3 to 1.5 m), and the furniture grew with the rooms. The island was reshaped round it: the castle grew north and west from the foot of the climb, the rest of the island moved with it (26 m east and 40 m south in the grid), the farm and the pool's walks sit west of the pool under the rock's south face, and the north-east upland reaches to the fracture notch beside the castle's rock.

## Known departures from the plan

Recorded stage by stage in [DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](DESIGN_DECISIONS.md); the ones still true:

- The wall towers stand 2.6 m out from the curtain's line, so the walls die into their drums and the walk's doorways fit in them. The keep's galleries stand at the walk's level less 6 cm, on a course line.
- The moat's outer bank is set out on the cell grid (its splays true diagonals, none more than half a metre off the plan); the weir and the culvert's mouth under the bridge are not modelled (out of sight under the bridge's south arch); the knolls' rock faces seen from below are still the cliff generator's broad facets.
- Each garden's side gate stands on the garden's cross axis and opens across the lawn to the cour (the plan put it on the north walk); the training yard's butts stand before the barracks and its pells by the curtain; the wall towers' drums bulge 0.5 m into the paddock and the training yard, so their fences stop at the drums.
- The great door's leaves are the shared 5.2 m 'great' class under the 10.5 m arch.
- The rock's ledges carry no pines: the cliff scenery is seeded from the layout, so unrelated layout edits reshuffle it.
- Closed for now: the keep's great chamber, map room and treasury; the wall walks.
