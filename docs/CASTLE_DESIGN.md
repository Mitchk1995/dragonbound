# Dragonspire Castle (castle v3) as built

Mitchell's October 3 decisions change this castle: a great keep (castle v4, being planned in `docs/blueprints/`), trim and kerbs cut from the same stone as the walls, round towers laid in flat stones that stand out, real stairs wherever the way climbs, and new grown trees. See "The new look and the great keep" in [DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](DESIGN_DECISIONS.md).

The castle stands on the home island's crown. Its plan is in `src/data/zoneMaps.ts`: `KEEP_BUILDINGS` for the enterable buildings, and the castle constants (`CURTAIN`, `SCREEN`, `GATE`, `POSTERN`, `WARD_GATES`, `TOWERS`, `DONJON`, `FOUNTAIN`, exported for the dev tools as `CASTLE_PLAN`) for the walls, towers, gates and the centrepiece. The same doors, partitions, stairs and furniture footprints drive rendering and collision. Coordinates are island cells; north is negative Z. A range "x a..b" gives cell edges, so the cells are a to b − 1.

## The idea

One great bailey, with no cross wall, laid out on the keep's axis.

- **Main axis, x = 66**, straight up the screen: the gate in the south wall, the avenue, the dragon fountain, the cour d'honneur between the mirrored west wing and barracks, the feast court and the hall's great door.
- **Cross axis, z = 78**: from a champion in a niche on the west wall, through the fountain, to the ward gate in the inner curtain and the stable court beyond.
- **Mirrored about the axis:** the bailey's two side walls (the west curtain and the inner curtain), the west wing and the barracks, the two pavilions framing the great door, the parterre's panels and walks, the south wall's towers and its two wall stairs.
- **Every walk joins another:** the paving inside the curtain is one connected network, and every lawn, garden and yard can be walked into from the gate. Tests hold all of this (`tests/keep.test.ts`).

## The outer works

- **Curtain:** six faces, clockwise from the north-west corner (31, 38), (50, 22.5), (101, 22.5), (120, 36), (120, 100), (31, 100): straight north, east, south and west faces with chamfered north-west and north-east corners and a square south-east corner (a corner tower), and the inner curtain on x = 101 from the north wall to the south wall. 2.2 thick, full height all round, the wall walk at +7 between two crenellated parapets at one height on every run: the outer one flush with the outer face, the inner one corbelled out over the bailey as a machicolation (a corbel table carrying a blue-grey course, the parapet standing on it flush with the corbels' fronts), both with merlons and open embrasures at one rhythm, so from the yards the walls carry the same corbelled, crenellated crown as their towers. At a tower the inner parapet, its course and corbel table run on into the drum. One banner hangs centred on the outer face of every run between towers (two on a run longer than about two tower spacings, none on the short runs beside the postern and the ward gates). Where it stands between the camera and the hero a soft upright window opens in it round them (a rounded rectangle feathered into the wall, the rest solid; Mitchell's pick D), like every tall thing on the island. Its merlons and corbels are spread evenly along each run, so a run dies cleanly into its towers' drums.
- **Towers:** thirteen round towers (the curtain stops at each drum). The six at the curtain's corners rise a stage higher than the rest, a gilt diamond frieze under their parapet and a blue-slate spire with a gilt finial and a pennant inside the merlon ring; the wall towers between them (midway along the east and west faces and the inner curtain, and two on the south face either side of the gate) rise a storey above the wall walk to a flat crenellated top paved as a compass rose. Their bands run on level with the curtain's string courses. Every tower stands on the crown's level, never down on the road or cliff beside it.
- **Gatehouse:** on the axis in the south face (66, 100): two D-towers (set 5.4 either side, so the arch and its hood stand clear between them, a gilt frieze under their parapets) standing out from the wall either side of a passage four cells wide under the ceremonial arch (the great door's twin: on both faces a deep ring of voussoirs alternating pale ashlar and blue-grey, flush on one radius, springing from moulded blue-grey imposts on pale jambs in the same plane, a blue-grey hood lying on the ring and a big pale keystone bearing the lord's shield; outside, the tympanum over the opening is deep blue with the gold diamond on a blue-grey lintel, the raised portcullis set back behind it with only its spikes showing), the passage paved in setts a little darker than the road between pale threshold stones, a machicolated, crenellated block rising a storey over the curtain between the drums, the lord's banner over the arch on the outer face and a flag on each drum (none over the passage, so the view up the avenue stays clear). With the flags on the two south-face towers either side, the gate front flies five in one symmetric composition.
- **Postern:** a narrow gate in the west wall (31, 53) under a pointed arch with a lantern either side, its blue leaves standing open in the reveal on the bailey side under a lintel and a deep blue tympanum filling the arch, steps out onto the belvedere.
- **Ward gates:** two gates in the inner curtain, each under a pointed arch with a hood mould, lanterns, blue leaves standing open in the reveal on the ward side under a lintel and a deep blue tympanum filling the arch, and a raised crenellated head bearing the lord's crest: off the feast court (z 45) and on the cross axis (z 78).
- **Wall walk:** every tower and the gatehouse's drums open onto the curtain's wall walk at a doorway (a single blue door in a dressed frame) wherever the walk comes to them. A slim round stair tower on the north-east wall at the head of the kitchen garden's walk (its door at its foot, a slate spire) climbs to it. The wall walk itself is not playable yet.
- **Donjon:** the round tower at the north-west (50, 33.5), radius 7 and 13 high, beside the residence range's west (dais) end with its drum's front on the hall's facade line, so it never bulges into the cour: west of the cour the feast court runs on before it to the bower's court, the mirror of its run east past the barracks, and the great door's court closes square on both sides. Crowned by the castle's great spire, four steep gabled lucarnes round it. It is solid until stage 2 opens it.

- **Door turret:** east of the great door a slim round stair turret in the donjon's language (cream ashlar, blue-grey bands, a gilt frieze, two lancets toward the court) stands against the range's front with a blue-slate spire a stage lower than the door tower's, answering the donjon's drum across the axis so the door is framed by two round masses.

## The approach

The approach road leaves the portal court north-west and climbs at one steady grade up a cutting in solid rock to a level landing under the castle's south-east corner (a parapet and lamps round it). There it turns west (the gatehouse is the castle's only way in; no gate stands across the ledge road) and runs level with the crown along the whole ledge under the south wall, a grass verge between it and the wall, ending on the axis in the middle of the gate terrace. A parapet runs unbroken along the road's open side from the landing (turning on a capped pier where the ledge meets it) and round the terrace, built in short lengths on one blue-grey coping line, the paving running in under its inner half to its foot, standing straight on the rock's lip with no built footing under it, the rock's masses rising to meet it; lamp posts stand inside it, and the terrace has the two real lamps. Below the ledge the spring breaks out of a cleft in a shoulder of rock (moss seated on the rock masses' tops, ivy trailing flat down the wet rock, a glossy wet band half again as wide as the fall behind the whole drop), falls onto a chunky shelf of rock merged into the face with a rounded, water-worn lip, and drops again in a wide fraying sheet, mist rising and foam rings spreading, into a round pool with reeds and a soft bank, and the stream runs on from it in one unbroken channel to the pond, and from the pond off the island's south edge in a fall into the Veil. A second spring breaks out at the brink of the crown's west buttress from a mossy cleft between rounded masses of the rock and falls the island's whole height into the Veil, mist drifting off it. The spring path ends at a round viewing terrace paved right across in radial flagstones inside its kerb, the path coming in over a threshold stone, a stone bench on the far side looking at the fall and a lamp either side of it on the grass. The climb's kerb walls start from a capped pier and end each run of grade in another, mirrored on both sides; its paving stops at their inner faces, and outside them is the cutting's rock, never road. The earth paths outside the castle are drawn along their centre lines, so their edges curve instead of stepping along the cells; the farm lane ends at the hay paddock's gate between stone piers.

## The bailey

Lawn everywhere a walk, court or yard does not pave it. There are no barrels, crates or carts lying about.

- **Avenue:** six wide, paved, from the feast court through the fountain plaza and the gate to the terrace, with paired lamp posts through the cour; inside the gate a broad paved arrival forecourt, framed by clipped yews and a pair of lamps, runs from the gate to the parterre's south walk, so the fountain is seen across open paving; banded at the gate's threshold and the head of the feast court.
- **Kerbs:** wherever a lawn meets the paving, and wherever gravel or beaten earth meets the flagstones or a lawn, a dressed blue-grey kerb edges it; round the fountain a ring kerb lies flush under the arcs of box.
- **Walks:** the terrace walk along the parterre's head, the cross walk, the west walk (the privy garden's central walk, then the parterre's west side), the east lane (the parterre's east side and the service lane), the south walk under the curtain, the wing walks either side of the cour, and short links to the barracks' east door and the postern.
- **Great parterre:** four lawn panels round the round fountain plaza (radius 9), set at the true midpoint between the gate and the great door, mirrored about both axes. Each is edged in low clipped box with openings onto the walks, its plaza side cut back as a concave arc with a bench facing the basin; a champion faces the avenue from a small paved square on the panel's statue walk (the walk passes him on both sides and runs on through the hedge openings, the box running right up to the walk's kerb so no corner of bare lawn is left), each set in a lozenge of clipped box (the embroidery), flower borders run inside the outer hedges between a blossom or gold tree in each outer corner, and clipped cones stand at the arc's ends. The fountain stands at the parterre's true centre on both axes; the cour north of it is the great door's own forecourt.
- **Dragon fountain:** the centrepiece at the crossing of the axes, facing the gate: a round basin with a moulded kerb round a clear pool over a dark floor, an octagonal pedestal of cream ashlar on a blue-grey plinth with a gilt band and a blue-grey coping, and on it the bronze dragon (Mitchell's pick A), a chunky, blocky sentinel sitting upright like a guardian lion, about 8 high: a few big polished castings, the haunches folded under, the deep chest upright with paler belly plates, straight thick forelegs planted on broad feet, the wings folded flat against its flanks, the tail curled round its side to a gold spade, the head held high with a square snout and gold horns, gold spines and claws, water pouring from its open jaws. Four small jets arc in from stone pedestals on the kerb's diagonals; foam, spray and spreading rings mark where the water lands. The pool reflects the dragon as a soft bronze glow at low, oblique looks and only its sky and teal from the play camera's height.
- **Cour d'honneur:** lawn panels either side of the avenue between the wings, each dressed in the parterre's language at a long scale: a low box border inset from its edges (open on the avenue side), flower ribbons down both sides inside it, and a line of three clipped standards (cone, ball, cone) on its long axis, mirrored across the avenue.
- **Great door:** under a pointed arch with dressed voussoirs, a hood mould and a carved tympanum, between two identical square pavilions standing forward of the hall's facade, each rising a storey above the parapet with a lancet on its two upper storeys (the ground storey is blank behind its champion); over the door the entrance tower rises to a blue-slate spire on the castle's axis; the hall's front has no other door on the court. The wings' doors onto their side walks have blue-grey hoods and a lantern either side.
- **Feast court:** two marble champions in the lord's tabard and two urns before the great door, the well on the kitchen door's axis and clipped balls either side of the kitchen door; it runs on through the north ward gate.
- **Orchard:** a paved walk up its middle from the feast court to a bench in a paved niche on its axis, clear of the corner tower and backed by a low box hedge, eight small fruit trees (one tree, turned the same way) in a true grid either side of it, each well clear of the walls.
- **West niche:** a champion facing down the cross walk, clipped cones either side, and a long flower border along the wall's foot either side of those.
- **Privy garden:** hedged from the parterre with its gate on the west walk; clipped standard trees in pairs either side of the walk with flower beds in the open between them and a mirrored pair of lamps on the walk's edges at each end; the postern's cross walk ends at a seat in an arched niche against the wing, answering the postern.
- **Bower:** a rose pergola (three pointed timber arches, climbing roses, a lantern) over a seat at the head of the west walk, standing on the paved court between clipped balls, a matched pair of clipped yew cones either side of it and the curtain's roses and ivy behind; the lawn behind the donjon is a grove of trees.
- **Belvedere:** a paved viewing terrace out on the lip of a headland level with the crown, reached by a short paved walk from the postern: on the brink an open balustrade (slender pale balusters, a slate rail) between two end piers each carrying a lantern, a gold-capped urn on a pier at each inner corner, kerbs where it meets the lawn on its flanks, a bench in the middle facing west over the Veil.
- **Kitchen garden:** four square quarters (5 by 5), each boxed in clipped box on all four sides (a square post at every corner) with two raised beds of one crop each (cabbages, lettuces, carrots, leeks, red cabbages), round a cross of walks meeting at a sundial on the ward's axis, a two-cell lane down each side (the north ward gate opening on a paved landing on the west one) and a gravel row parting it from the training yard's fence; the corner tower's foot stands in a margin of lawn clear of the north-east quarter and the east lane; the north walk leads to a herb court under the north-east wall (a raised bed of lavender either side, clipped cones at the stair tower's door), the south walk goes on through the training yard's gate.
- **Training yard:** beaten earth running wall to wall between the inner curtain and the east curtain, centred on the ward's axis between two matching wall towers face to face at its foot, lighter worn lanes and one gravel lane down its middle, fenced from the kitchen garden with one gate on that axis. The lists (north): four pells in the lord's blue and gold either side of the lane and a weapon rack with blue shields flat against the fence either side of the gate. One fence across the yard with its gate on the axis is the shooting line; three butts with blue pennants stand at the range's far end before the stable range, the middle one closing the lane.
- **Stable court:** before the stables, standing free with a lane past either end to the training yard: the stable door on the court's axis and in the middle of the range under a pointed arch of voussoirs (the castle's side-door arch), the hay loft's door over it closed by a pair of blue shutters strapped in gold, an arched stall opening either side with a half-door and a horse looking out, flowers under them; the trough before the stable door, a blossom tree in a square planter either side of the axis.
- **Paddock:** behind its fence, the gate between stone piers on the stables' axis (its leaf swung flat back against the fence), the trough just inside it, the field shelter (stone, flat-roofed behind a parapet) against the east wall, two blocky horses (squared barrel, straight block legs, long square heads) in the north half clear of the walls, a fence across its foot short of the curtain.

## Buildings

Every castle building has a flat roof with a crenellated parapet (no pitched roofs), its leads a designed surface: blue lead in a two-tone diamond chequer inside a pale stone band and a gilt fillet. Chimneys rise above the parapet; a louvred stone lantern with a slate cap lets the hall's hearth smoke out; the wings carry two glazed lanterns with slate caps, a roof garden of box in planters either side of them and a stair turret with a blue door. Side doors are pointed arches: pale jambs to the springing, a ring of voussoirs alternating pale and blue-grey closing on a keystone, one blue-grey hood onto label stops meeting on an apex stone, a pair of the lord's blue pointed leaves filling the whole arch with an iron ring at hand height, a lantern either side, and a long house banner between the floor-line course and the parapet; no pilaster ever stands in a door or window. Windows are tall pointed lancets (a two-centred arch cut into the wall, clear blue-tinted glass, no mullion, a hood mould following the arch onto an apex stone), few and placed symmetrically (on the court wings one between the doors, one over it and one high at each end); the donjon's are the same.

## Stairs and floors

A stair is a straight flight inside a room (`stairs` on a building). On the ground floor its foot row takes you up; upstairs its head row takes you down; the rest of the flight and the stairwell are solid. You arrive beside it on the other floor, outside the trigger. A short fade switches the player's height, collision grid, camera and pet. Rooms open to the roof are `voids` on the upper floor: no floor there, and the hall below stays in view from the galleries.

The residence range's stair climbs north along the screens passage's west side to the minstrel gallery.

## The castle rock

The crown stands on a mountain of natural rock, and every cliff on the island (and in the other zones' outdoor rock) is built the same way:

- **Beds:** the rock is cut on natural bedding planes at irregular spacing (mostly tall beds of two to six, now and then a thin one) that dip and roll across the land by up to about 20 degrees, so ledges run at different heights along a face and no two cliffs step alike. The mine's cave walls keep their even, stacked strata.
- **Weathered faces:** above the foot every face is pushed back into the rock by different amounts along the cliff and up it, standing out as buttresses, falling back into bays, leaning out over the bed below or cut back under the one above. The foot stays on its cell edge and the rock at the island's edge stays put over the underside.
- **The look (Mitchell's pick, style C, October 2):** dark blue-grey rock heavily softened by moss, rounded weathered shoulders and big irregular masses of different sizes, pines and ferns on the ledges and the lip, waterfalls with mist at their foot. Never rows of same-size columns, ribs, terrace rings or battlement-like caps.
- **Paint:** big upright fractured facets, each catching the light from its own side, long vertical joints, a few broken bedding lines and the odd patch of fractures; dark rain streaks run down the faces and ochre and cool stains drift across them; on the keep's lush rock moss spreads over most tops and down the rounded shoulders, elsewhere only in broad patches on the flattest ledges, never as a line along every bed.
- **Rock masses, boulders and scree:** a kit of ten weathered rock masses (rounded, faceted lumps, each its own profile: low domes, tall rounded blocks, slab-topped ledges, leaning knuckles) stands out of every face taller than a metre, overlapped at very different sizes and turns: a few huge shoulders, many middling lumps, small knuckles slumped at their feet, and on the tallest faces more masses bedded higher up, so the whole height breaks up. Each is fitted to the ground: shrunk until it never stands out over a road, paving or a lawn at the foot, lowered until it never rises through the ground on top. Moss drapes over every crown in a soft, lumpy sheet. Boulders sized to the face lie fallen at its foot and scree spills a little way out over the grass. None crowds a fall.
- **Plants:** grass grows wherever the rock lies flat, on the ledges and along the top lip; on the masses' front shoulders the odd pine, ferns (arching fronds set with pairs of blocky leaflets shortening to the tip) and moss cushions; small trees only in a few planted pockets, bare rock between (never a row along every ledge).
- **The road's edge:** the ledge road's parapet stands straight on the rock's lip, no built footing between road and rock; the masses' tops follow a broad noise along the face, some rounding off right under the lip, others stopping well short. The ramp's kerbs stand on a honey retaining face. Cliff lips sit just above the turf, and the lawn rolls over every lip in a fringe of leaning tufts.
- **Masses:** on the lush keep the faces are only lightly cut back, so the rock masses shape them; even faces a storey or less high take a broken rim of masses, so no grass plateau ends in a bare cut. Every face carries one big weathering gradient, warmer and darker at its damp foot and cooler and lighter toward the crown, and moss lies only in broad patches on faces that truly face up (laid from above, never tracing a bed). On the west a buttress of the crown runs out to the island's edge, breaking the face's tiers; on the east the shoulder runs close under the crown so the two cliffs merge into one broken massif. The island's hanging underside is the same rock masses turned upside down, of very different depths, none under a fall.
- **Spurs:** at irregular intervals along the foot of every tall face a spur of the same rock runs out over the ground below, high where it leaves the face and stepping down to its nose.
- **The island's side:** a sheer skirt of the same rock drops from the very edge of the land (ground or cliff top) to below the underside, so the island is one closed mass with no sky showing through; the underside's hanging columns and core are the cliff's darker rock. Slivers of land sticking out into the void break off. No rock floats beside the castle: the drifting islets are two loose clusters far out off the south-west, well below the land (never in the castle's silhouette from any camera), each an inverted cone of the cliff rock under a domed grass cap with tufts over its rim and roots hanging under it, bobbing slowly. The castle rock wraps all four sides: on the north and east the crown hugs the curtain and drops in a scarp to the shoulder. Ledges partway down a face are bare rock (turf only on the top), cut by gullies into separate buttresses. Roads and terraces over the rock are walled by the parapet, which ends in capped piers; the climb from the court is kerbed by a low sloping wall on both sides and eases level onto the landing.

## Grass, water and sky

The Veil is lit at the golden hour: a warm, low sun, a lilac sky warming to peach at the horizon, a sea of soft cloud below the island's edge, a bright sky bounce so shade stays readable. No stars.


Every grassy cell on the island grows a continuous shell lawn: clipped short with mower stripes inside the castle, taller in the meadows, where the blades are slender (two interleaved fields of them keep the sward full). The meadow at the rock's foot rolls gently, easing to level ground round the plots, paths, rock and water. The field plots each grow one crop in three rows on ridged soil (cabbages, lettuces, carrots, leeks or red cabbages, turning through the grid).
