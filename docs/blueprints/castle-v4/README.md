# Castle v4: the Great Keep

The castle becomes one grand composition around a great keep. At the head of the bailey, on the line from the gate, a massive square keep stands with its back in the moat. It rises 28 m to its battlements, and its four corner turrets reach blue spires at 44 m, well above anything else (the chapel's flèche, the next highest, tops out at 27 m). A grand stair climbs to an 8 m great door under one showpiece window, and the great hall and a chapel stand lower on either side under steep blue slate roofs, the hall's lantern and the chapel's slender flèche on their ridges, between the low battlemented kitchen and solar. Below, the bailey is a mirror image: walled gardens, then the stables and the barracks facing each other across the dragon fountain, then a paddock three times today's size and the training yard. Twelve evenly spaced towers ring the walls. A moat rings all four sides, widening into a basin behind the keep, whose back rises straight out of the water; two springs feed it from the rock behind the keep, and it runs round both sides to the gate, is crossed by a bridge from the walled ledge road and pours out under the gate terrace as a waterfall to the stream. The rock's edge is natural all round, wandering in and out, with a knoll at the south-west corner for the lookout; the built work sits on it, the ledge's outer wall following the brink in straight runs with turf between it and the paved way, and only the gate bastion and the stair are squared masonry. From the portal court a stone stair of four flights with level landings climbs the rock's south-east corner to the ledge road, which has a wall on both sides. Every part is built as a mason would build it: no slopes too steep to walk, no drops without a wall, every terrace held by a retaining wall, every bridge on arches.

Inside, every building the hero enters works with its outside: each door is the same door from both sides, each window lights a room, each chimney stands over a hearth, each upper floor has its stair. The keep's throne hall rises 18 m to the great chamber so the great door and the showpiece window stand clear; the stables have six stalls either side of an aisle with the hay loft over them, and the barracks mirror them.

Approved by the owner on October 3, with these choices decided:

- **Roofs:** steep blue slate on the great hall and the chapel; the kitchen and the solar keep flat roofs behind battlements, so the range climbs from their battlements over the two slate roofs to the keep, and the ridges (20.9 m) answer the corner towers' spire tips (21.5).
- **The moat** rings all four sides; behind the keep a rim of natural rock carries its north bank, the crown and the island grown up to 4.5 m north for it. No one walks the rim.
- **The keep** stands 28 m to its battlements, the turrets 34.5 m, the spires 44 m. The play camera shows its base, the stair and the great door; its full height shows on the approach, in the overview and on the title screen.
- **One waterfall to the stream**, under the gate terrace, where the moat empties (not two either side of the bridge); today's west fall off the brink into the Veil stays, fed by a sluice.
- **The belvedere and the little west gate go:** the moat takes their ground.
- **The lookout** is a walled terrace on a natural knoll, its wall following the rim; the landing on the south-east follows the rock the same way, squarer where the stair is built against the dressed rock face.
- **The gate:** today's gatehouse stays as it is, with flags on flat drums, so the keep's spires rise over it seen from the farm.
- **Inside the keep:** a throne hall with the lord's high seat, open 18 m to the great chamber's floor, with galleries on three sides at the wall walk's level. The great chamber, map room and treasury above stay closed for now.
- **A chapel and the lord's solar** are new: the chapel balances the great hall on the other side of the keep.
- **The farm** moves 8 m south to make room for the waterfall and the bigger rock.
