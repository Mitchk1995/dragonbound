# Castle v4: the Great Keep

The castle becomes one grand composition around a great keep. At the head of the bailey, on the line from the gate, a massive square keep stands with its back on the north cliff. It rises 28 m to its battlements, and its four corner turrets reach blue spires at 44 m, twice the height of anything else. A grand stair climbs to an 8 m great door under one showpiece window, and the great hall and a chapel stand lower on either side. Below, the bailey is a mirror image: walled gardens, then the stables and the barracks facing each other across the dragon fountain, then a paddock three times today's size and the training yard. Twelve evenly spaced towers ring the walls. A moat surrounds them, crossed by a bridge from the walled ledge road, and pours out under the gate terrace as a waterfall to the stream.

Choices you might want to change:

- **Roofs:** every castle building keeps its flat roof behind battlements, as decided before. The painting gives the hall and the chapel steep navy slate roofs, and they could have them.
- **The keep's height:** it could come down a few metres. The play camera never shows it whole, only its base, the stair and the great door. Its full height shows on the approach, in the overview and on the title screen.
- **One waterfall or two:** there is one fall, under the gate terrace, where the moat empties. The painting has two, either side of the bridge.
- **The belvedere:** the moat takes its ground, so it goes, and the little west gate with it. A footbridge over the moat could bring both back.
- **The gate:** today's gatehouse stays as it is, with flags on flat drums. Spires on it would hide the keep's spires when you look up from the farm.
- **Inside the keep:** a throne hall with the lord's high seat, two storeys high, with galleries round it. The floors above stay closed for now.
- **A chapel and the lord's solar** are new: the chapel balances the great hall on the other side of the keep.
- **The farm** moves 8 m south to make room for the waterfall and the bigger rock.
