# Trees

Part of the design plan; the index and the current direction are in [DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](../DESIGN_DECISIONS.md). The October 3 verdict on the six woodcutting trees is recorded with the text verdict in [ui.md](ui.md) ("Text and trees, verdicts"). Newer decisions take precedence over older ones. Record each new decision here, dated, in the same session it is made.

## Where the trees stand

- **Trees:** the grown oak and the common tree are built and approved with their bark (Mitchell, "looks good", October 3; see "Trees (October 3)" below); the willow, maple, yew and magic tree are built and every zone's woods and lawns now grow the ladder, waiting on Mitchell's verdict from pictures. The bushes are grown the same way and approved (October 4, below). The dead ash is grown the same way too; Mitchell rejected its bark and colours, and its new bark waits on his verdict.

## Bushes and dead ash (October 4)

- **Bushes grown like the trees: approved** ("yes, good"). **Dead ash grown then broken: rejected** for its bark and colour only: "texture is completely off on the dead trees doesnt look anything close to right with the bark and colors". The shapes stay; the bark and colours are being redone from real dead-ash reference.

## The pick (October 3)

- **Trees: pick B, "Natural".** Redo the trees at true size, no longer blocky, as the woodcutting ladder in RuneScape's
  order: tree, oak, willow, maple, yew, magic. After seeing the first grown oak he chose the natural, realistic look
  ("i kinda like the realistic one") and dropped the cartoony and mixed versions planned for comparison. The rest of the
  ladder follows in the same style. Cartoony and blocky stay for the characters, enemies and NPCs, because that is what
  Claude models well. Each species is grown from one recipe in three or four shapes, and every placed tree is turned
  and sized a little differently. That way a wood never looks copy-pasted, yet an oak still reads as an oak at a glance,
  which woodcutting needs (Claude's proposal, October 3; Mitchell had wondered whether they should all be the same).
  Everything else should get a nice texture too, the crops and plants included.

## Building the ladder (October 3)

- **Trees are redone as natural, true-size trees** (Mitchell, castle round 5, on the landing's trees): bigger, actual tree size, moving away from blocky completely, and laid out for woodcutting in a ladder like RuneScape's (tree, oak, willow, maple, yew, magic). From two painted rows he picked B, "Natural": closer to real trees in proportion and leaf detail, softly painted, still clean and readable from above, made with Claude's own taste rather than copied from the painting. Everything gets a proper texture.
- **The oak comes first, as the proof** (the 'natural' tree style, `src/world/trees.ts`): a mature oak about 11 m tall with a crown about 13 m across, five and a half times the 2 m hero; its trunk, flared roots and crooked limbs grown as one continuous surface with furrowed bark and smooth collars at every fork; a crown of painted sprays of lobed oak leaves, shaded in soft masses (lit tops, cool undersides, a darker inner crown) and swaying in the wind; three seeded variants, about 7,100 to 7,900 triangles each. A wood of them is thinned to one oak every 7.5 m, with no bush under their crowns. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:oak`.
- **Natural, not cartoony** (Mitchell, October 3, from the first oak pictures): he likes the realistic oak; cartoony is for the characters. The three-looks test is dropped. The oak was polished (long vertical furrows with real relief instead of a net of scales, nine leaf-spray designs, a less saturated crown with stronger light and shade, leaf cards that no longer show as slivers edge on), and the **common tree** followed as the ladder's first rung: about 7.5 m tall, a slim straight trunk under a rounded crown of oval leaves, about 3,600 to 3,800 triangles. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:grown:tree`. Willow, maple, yew and magic come next, from the same generator; the forests and garden trees are not switched to grown trees yet.
- **The whole ladder, in the game's woods** (Claude, October 3, for Mitchell to judge from pictures): the **willow** (about 9 m, a crown wider than tall, a stout often-leaning trunk forking low, its branches arching over and letting fall a curtain of long strands of narrow leaves to just above the hero's head, light silvery yellow-green, criss-crossed diamond bark), the **maple** (about 10.5 m, a broad full dome of lobed leaves on a straight grey plated trunk; green, or red, orange and gold where the zone wears autumn), the **yew** (about 9 m, a dark dense evergreen of needle sprays on a massive fluted red-brown flaking trunk splitting low into many limbs) and the **magic tree** (about 11 m, the one fantasy: a slim, gently leaning pale silver-blue trunk with faintly glowing veins, long limbs sweeping up into an open crown of glowing teal leaves, still shaped like a real tree). Each is grown from the same generator in three or four shapes (a leaning willow, one forking low, a tall narrow one; an upright and a spreading maple; a conical yew and a squat many-stemmed one; a vase-shaped and a tall slender magic tree), 4,000 to 7,900 triangles each. Bark and leaves are sourced: the Codex bark and leaf sprays from the October 3 batch, made tileable and small by `tools/bark_textures.py` (leaf atlases in `public/textures/leaves/`). The grown trees are now the game's trees (the 'natural' style): each zone says which species grow in place of its broadleaves and conifers (`ZoneTheme.woods`): on the castle island common trees and oaks with willows by the water and a few maples; in the Wyrmwood Foothills yews and autumn maples with oaks and willows by the water; in the Sunken Ruin willows, magic trees and old yews; yews in the mine. Species stand in groves, willows mostly by water; dead ash and bushes kept their block models (until October 4, below). Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:ladder`. On the Foothills' heaviest views the trees cost about 0.5 to 1 ms more of the GPU's frame than the block trees did (frames stay about 3.5 to 5 ms).
- **Textures are sourced, not hand-painted in code** (Mitchell, October 3, on the oak's bark up close: the limbs looked like bones and a smooth band ringed the foot): textures come from image generation with Codex or from free CC0 libraries. For the bark, a photo-scanned CC0 bark (Poly Haven) and a softly painted bark generated with Codex were compared in the game; the painted one is used (deep oak furrows that sit beside the painted stone; the photo read noisy and its knots repeated round the trunk). Each tree kind has its own tileable bark and relief (`public/textures/bark/`, sources in its `LICENSES.md`, made by `tools/bark_textures.py`), wrapped round every limb and laid on from the sides over every fork and root collar, so the bark runs on unbroken from the roots to the twigs. Mitchell approved the oak and its bark ("looks good", October 3).
- **Roots that go into the ground, and no bare sticks** (Mitchell, October 3, tree notes round 1, `D:\dragonbound-archive\notes\trees-round1`: the magic tree's, the yew's and the willow's roots looked weird, and a random stick poked out of the yew's crown). Every species' roots are now ridges of the trunk's own foot: they swell out of the bark from about knee height and run down into the ground within a short way, round and thick to where they meet it, with no thin spikes lying on the grass and no ends above it; under the ground they draw in, so on a slope of up to about 1 in 2.5 the downhill side stays buried. A branch that would carry nothing but a lone tuft at its tip, or a few leaves off on their own, is left off, and one whose outer stretch is bare is cut back to its leaves (the yew's stick was one). The crowns are otherwise as before. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:roots`.

## The dead ash and the bushes (October 4)

- **Grown, not blocks** (Claude, October 4, for Mitchell to judge from pictures; job J8 of the methods audit): the dead ash and the bushes are grown the way the approved trees are, from the same generator, and stand in the natural style's woods in place of their block models. A **dead ash** is an ash grown as a living tree (about 8.5 m, a straight trunk under an open crown of crooked limbs whose shoots turn up at their tips) and then killed: its leaves gone, its top snapped off, some limbs snapped partway out, most small branches fallen and many of those left broken short or at their tips, every break a blunt, ragged end of splinters, its roots and its girth as they grew. Its first bark was bare, weathered silver-grey wood charred up its foot (redone, below). Three shapes: a tall snag with its top snapped high, a squat one forked low into spreading limbs, and one leaning a little, 4 to 7 m tall, about 1,500 to 1,600 triangles. A **bush** is grown from the ground up: five to seven stems out of one root crown under the soil, fanning up and out, their shoots reaching a low dome that leaf sprays clothe right down to the grass (broad, glossy oval leaves). Three shapes: a round mound, a low wide one and a tall upright one with its stems showing at its foot, 1.2 to 1.9 m tall and 2 to 3 m across at their own size (the woods set them at half to full size, and a quarter lower), about 1,500 to 2,800 triangles. The bush leaves are sourced from the Codex library, made by `tools/bark_textures.py`. In a wood the dead ash keep their own small patches before the living trees round them take the rest, each with room round it, and none stands with its foot in a boulder or a lava-rim stone; the Foothills grow about fifteen, Cinderwing's lair about thirteen. The frame cost of the Foothills' and the lair's woods is unchanged. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:bushes` (and `trees:bushes:angles` for every shape from four sides).
- **The dead ash's bark, redone** (Mitchell, October 4, approving the bushes but not the dead ash: "texture is completely off on the dead trees doesnt look anything close to right with the bark and colors"; its shapes, sizes and placing stay; redone by Claude for him to judge from pictures): it wears an ash's grey-brown bark, narrow ridges interlacing round long furrows (a photographed CC0 bark from Poly Haven, toned to the painted world, as fine as the oak's and the common tree's), still on nearly all of its trunk low down, its damp foot mossy in the furrows. Higher up the bark has loosened and fallen away in long patches running with the grain, and it is off most of its limbs and branches and round every break, baring smooth, weathered silver-grey wood (from the Codex library); the bark's torn edge shows its paler inner bark, the wood just past it is stained darker, and every break's splintered end is paler, fibrous wood. The char is dropped: black char round the foot would hide the very bark a dead ash keeps longest. Each tree's patches are its own. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:ash` (beside the oak and the common tree, close up, and in the Foothills' wood and Cinderwing's lair).
