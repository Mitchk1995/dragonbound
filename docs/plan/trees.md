# Trees

Part of the design plan; the index and the current direction are in [DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](../DESIGN_DECISIONS.md). The October 3 verdict on the six woodcutting trees is recorded with the text verdict in [ui.md](ui.md) ("Text and trees, verdicts"). Newer decisions take precedence over older ones. Record each new decision here, dated, in the same session it is made.

## Where the trees stand

- **Trees:** the grown oak and the common tree are built and approved with their bark (Mitchell, "looks good", October 3; see "Trees (October 3)" below); the willow, maple, yew and magic tree are built and every zone's woods and lawns now grow the ladder, waiting on Mitchell's verdict from pictures.

## The pick (October 3)

- **Trees: pick B, "Natural".** Redo the trees at true size, no longer blocky, as the woodcutting ladder in RuneScape's
  order: tree, oak, willow, maple, yew, magic. After seeing the first grown oak he chose the natural, realistic look
  ("i kinda like the realistic one") and dropped the cartoony and mixed versions planned for comparison. The rest of the
  ladder follows in the same style. Cartoony and blocky stay for the characters, enemies and NPCs, because that is what
  Claude models well. Each species is grown from one recipe in three or four shapes, and every placed tree is turned
  and sized a little differently. That way a wood never looks copy-pasted, yet an oak still reads as an oak at a glance,
  which woodcutting needs (Claude's proposal, October 3; Mitchell had wondered whether they should all be the same).
  Everything else should get a nice texture too, the crops and plants included.

## Building the ladder (October 3)

- **Trees are redone as natural, true-size trees** (Mitchell, castle round 5, on the landing's trees): bigger, actual tree size, moving away from blocky completely, and laid out for woodcutting in a ladder like RuneScape's (tree, oak, willow, maple, yew, magic). From two painted rows he picked B, "Natural": closer to real trees in proportion and leaf detail, softly painted, still clean and readable from above, made with Claude's own taste rather than copied from the painting. Everything gets a proper texture.
- **The oak comes first, as the proof** (the 'natural' tree style, `src/world/trees.ts`): a mature oak about 11 m tall with a crown about 13 m across, five and a half times the 2 m hero; its trunk, flared roots and crooked limbs grown as one continuous surface with furrowed bark and smooth collars at every fork; a crown of painted sprays of lobed oak leaves, shaded in soft masses (lit tops, cool undersides, a darker inner crown) and swaying in the wind; three seeded variants, about 7,100 to 7,900 triangles each. A wood of them is thinned to one oak every 7.5 m, with no bush under their crowns. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:oak`.
- **Natural, not cartoony** (Mitchell, October 3, from the first oak pictures): he likes the realistic oak; cartoony is for the characters. The three-looks test is dropped. The oak was polished (long vertical furrows with real relief instead of a net of scales, nine leaf-spray designs, a less saturated crown with stronger light and shade, leaf cards that no longer show as slivers edge on), and the **common tree** followed as the ladder's first rung: about 7.5 m tall, a slim straight trunk under a rounded crown of oval leaves, about 3,600 to 3,800 triangles. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:grown:tree`. Willow, maple, yew and magic come next, from the same generator; the forests and garden trees are not switched to grown trees yet.
- **The whole ladder, in the game's woods** (Claude, October 3, for Mitchell to judge from pictures): the **willow** (about 9 m, a crown wider than tall, a stout often-leaning trunk forking low, its branches arching over and letting fall a curtain of long strands of narrow leaves to just above the hero's head, light silvery yellow-green, criss-crossed diamond bark), the **maple** (about 10.5 m, a broad full dome of lobed leaves on a straight grey plated trunk; green, or red, orange and gold where the zone wears autumn), the **yew** (about 9 m, a dark dense evergreen of needle sprays on a massive fluted red-brown flaking trunk splitting low into many limbs) and the **magic tree** (about 11 m, the one fantasy: a slim, gently leaning pale silver-blue trunk with faintly glowing veins, long limbs sweeping up into an open crown of glowing teal leaves, still shaped like a real tree). Each is grown from the same generator in three or four shapes (a leaning willow, one forking low, a tall narrow one; an upright and a spreading maple; a conical yew and a squat many-stemmed one; a vase-shaped and a tall slender magic tree), 4,000 to 7,900 triangles each. Bark and leaves are sourced: the Codex bark and leaf sprays from the October 3 batch, made tileable and small by `tools/bark_textures.py` (leaf atlases in `public/textures/leaves/`). The grown trees are now the game's trees (the 'natural' style): each zone says which species grow in place of its broadleaves and conifers (`ZoneTheme.woods`): on the castle island common trees and oaks with willows by the water and a few maples; in the Wyrmwood Foothills yews and autumn maples with oaks and willows by the water; in the Sunken Ruin willows, magic trees and old yews; yews in the mine. Species stand in groves, willows mostly by water; dead ash and bushes keep their block models. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:ladder`. On the Foothills' heaviest views the trees cost about 0.5 to 1 ms more of the GPU's frame than the block trees did (frames stay about 3.5 to 5 ms).
- **Textures are sourced, not hand-painted in code** (Mitchell, October 3, on the oak's bark up close: the limbs looked like bones and a smooth band ringed the foot): textures come from image generation with Codex or from free CC0 libraries. For the bark, a photo-scanned CC0 bark (Poly Haven) and a softly painted bark generated with Codex were compared in the game; the painted one is used (deep oak furrows that sit beside the painted stone; the photo read noisy and its knots repeated round the trunk). Each tree kind has its own tileable bark and relief (`public/textures/bark/`, sources in its `LICENSES.md`, made by `tools/bark_textures.py`), wrapped round every limb and laid on from the sides over every fork and root collar, so the bark runs on unbroken from the roots to the twigs. Mitchell approved the oak and its bark ("looks good", October 3).
- **Roots that go into the ground, and no bare sticks** (Mitchell, October 3, tree notes round 1, `D:\dragonbound-archive\notes\trees-round1`: the magic tree's, the yew's and the willow's roots looked weird, and a random stick poked out of the yew's crown). Every species' roots are now ridges of the trunk's own foot: they swell out of the bark from about knee height and run down into the ground within a short way, round and thick to where they meet it, with no thin spikes lying on the grass and no ends above it; under the ground they draw in, so on a slope of up to about 1 in 2.5 the downhill side stays buried. A branch that would carry nothing but a lone tuft at its tip, or a few leaves off on their own, is left off, and one whose outer stretch is bare is cut back to its leaves (the yew's stick was one). The crowns are otherwise as before. Seen with `npm run inspect -- trees:roots`.
