## Where the characters stand

- **Characters** come after the castle and stay blocky and modular. The work is on branch `art/character-polish` (stages A–D, plus part of the first critique round). Re-create its worktree from that branch to continue.

## October 4

- **Ember Cultist's head: the horned iron mask, no hood** (Mitchell's pick, October 4, decision page, over a hood made as cloth; the two were offered after the fourth hood was turned down: "think of a new idea, you cannot model hoods at all"). A dark horned iron mask (ember eyes in slanted slits) on an iron casque, all one shape, and a short mantle on her shoulders rising into a stiff gold-edged collar behind the head; the robe and gold trim as before, flat colours until the texture pass. His one note: "little bit of an issue on the gold that goes around the masked ones robe collar as the red kinda pokes through and its not continuous". Now the collar's gold is one continuous edge: one rolled binding from the clasp up one front edge, round the collar's top and down the other, joined to the clasp, with the cloth cut back to it, so no red shows through it or between pieces from any side, idle, walking, casting or hit. The mantle is made as cloth by `cloth.py` (a low cage shaped by Mirror, creased Subdivision and Shrinkwrap over a stand-in of her shoulders; a pinned cloth simulation with fixed settings, so a re-export gives the same cloth; baked, made symmetric, cut to facets by Decimate and thickened by Solidify); the cloth hood is gone.
- **No leg shows through a skirt any more** (in the game, awaiting Mitchell's verdict). At the end of each stride the thigh pushed through the chain and leather skirts (up to about 8 cm; more when hit or swinging a sword while stepping), and through the tunic and every other armour skirt too. Now, like a LEGO minifigure, the legs hinge under the hips at the tunic's hem, the tops of the thighs rounded inside it, and everything below the belt (the tunic's skirt, every armour's skirt and its flaps, tassets and tabard) stays level with the legs while the body leans over it; a sword swing turns the whole hero. The skirts keep their approved looks: their tops round up under the belt, the leather flaps and the plate's tassets start a little up behind the belt and the tabard a little over it, so no gap opens as the belt moves, and the legs swing a touch further and step a touch quicker, so the stride covers the same ground. Under armour the tunic's skirt and belt come off (their hem used to peek out under the leather, chain and Wyrmbone). Checked for every outfit through the whole stride, hurt and in every attack (`skirtcheck.py`). The goblin's loincloth and the kobold's hips still let a thigh through (their own models, not changed here); these are the hips the shared minifigure body (below) can carry to every humanoid, so no robe or skirt lets a leg through.

## October 3

- **Characters, enemies and NPCs are not locked** (October 3): "we could improve the designs we have too". They stay blocky and modular; the concept sheets are fresh takes to improve on.
- **Characters stay blocky and modular, like Lego.** Mitchell knows complex organic shapes are Claude's weak spot when
  built from pieces; trees are where we leave blocks behind, grown as one piece.
- **Concept sheets redone** (October 3). The first Codex enemy and character sheets were rejected (no references, weak prompts); the redo attached each in-game model and kept the blocky box construction. Verdicts (October 3, decision page): hero, Goblin Grunt, Kobold Slinger, Ember Cultist and Cinder Priest approved and now being built. The hero "kinda like it tbh": all equipment must be refitted to the new body and the gauntlets to his hands. The priest's staff loses the odd rectangular block on top. Drakeling rejected (the brow covers the eye so it looks like an eye on the side and the front; wings not placed properly on the back) and Cinderwing rejected for the same eye-and-brow problem ("pretty good" otherwise); both are being redrawn. Area paintings are for mood only, not layout. The interface art sheet is approved as a direction.
- **Five character sheets approved and built** (October 3): the hero, Goblin Grunt, Kobold Slinger, Ember Cultist and Cinder Priest (`D:\dragonbound-archive\codex\concepts2`). Mitchell: every gear piece must fit the new hero, gauntlets included; the Cinder Priest's staff loses the odd block on top for a proper flame head. The hero keeps his body's sizes so all gear still fits, and the starting outfit's extras (collar, strap, pauldron, bracers, boots) come off under the gear that covers them; the sword on his back was left out, since the sword is always in his hand. The Cinder Priest now has his own model, a head taller than his cultists. The redrawn drakeling and Cinderwing sheets were approved the same day and built.
- **Hands are one-piece LEGO hands, never thumbs; arms get elbows** (October 3, rejecting the round 2 characters). Mitchell: "no thumbs look bad on this type of hand, don't ever add them… it has to be all one shape like a lego hand", and the bow draw "needs an elbow obviously". Claude's brief had asked for a thumb, which caused the miss. Now in the game: every humanoid hand (hero, cultists, priest, goblin, Warden, Quartermaster) is one chunky C-clip with a hole and no thumb, and whatever it holds runs through the hole (sword grip, club, staff, bow, the drawn string); gloves and gauntlets of every tier are the same C a size up. Every humanoid arm has an upper arm, forearm and elbow: arms rest a little bent; staffs are carried upright with the forearm forward and stay upright when raised in the cast; a bow is carried plumb and out from the body, since its limbs curve in and would catch the striding leg; the raised sword comes up in line with the forearm; and the bow draw bends the elbow, out to the side, to bring the string hand to the jaw beside the cheek, with nothing passing through the chest (the chunky arm is too short to reach the chin itself). Armour sleeves gained elbow guards so a bent arm never shows a gap. Every character and weapon is checked in every pose for arms or held things cutting into bodies; only the goblin's raised club arm passing behind its big ear remains.
- **Hands and elbows, verdicts** (October 3, decision page). Hero: "yes, good: its ok but we should maybe fix what you mentioned", so the bow draw gets a side-on archer stance to bring the string hand to the chin, and the bow is carried angled down and forward instead of upright. Quartermaster approved. Ember Cultist: no; the hood (third time) "doesnt go up far enough… doesnt come over like a hood properly… back too far". Goblin Grunt: no; the chest strap "isnt attached to anything" and "doesnt go over shoulder", and his shoulder pad floats off the shoulder. Warden: no; "the chest emblem thing is clipping the beard and the hood kinda looks bad". Notes in `D:\dragonbound-archive\notes\minions-round3`.
- **Every humanoid on one minifigure body (October 4, agreed in chat).** Every humanoid matches the hero's style the way LEGO does it. All of them share one minifigure body (torso, hips, legs, arms with elbows, C-hands, with short legs for short races). Each gets its identity from its own head, headgear, hair or beard, colours, robe or skirt pieces and accessories, so every gear piece fits every humanoid. Creatures such as the dragons stay brick-built creatures. First proof: the goblin and the cultist rebuilt on the body (with their October 3 fixes), shown beside the current ones as one pick. The Warden's hood and emblem fixes wait for that pick.
- **Characters round 2: hands, hood, staff hold, arms** (October 3, notes in `D:\dragonbound-archive\notes\characters-round2`). Mitchell: "better, but the arms might be clipping to the sides as well on some"; the hand "should all be one piece like a glove", the raised sword wasn't held right, the cultist's staff hold looked weird and its hood was bent back at the top "like it's balding". Fixes: every hand of the hero's kind (hero, cultist, priest, goblin, Warden, Quartermaster) is one solid fist with the thumb across its front, the sword's grip closing in it (raised, the blade comes out of the fist in line with the forearm); the cultist's hood now covers the head in a rounded crown, as in the concept, with a small soft peak tipping forward over the face (a first, taller version read as a gnome's cone); the cult staffs run upright through the fist and stay upright in the raised hand when casting (they used to swing down behind the head); and no arm cuts into a body any more: the arms hang a little out from the sides, the robed and goblin/kobold arms sit against their bodies' sides, and body armour keeps its sides and sleeves on one line (the tunic's own sleeves come off under it). Checked for every character and pose by `fitcheck.py` `arm_clip_all()`. Two overlaps remain by design: the bow's draw arm reaches across the chest to the chin (no elbow to bend), and the goblin's raised club arm passes behind its big ear. (The fist with its thumb and the draw arm across the chest were replaced the same day by the LEGO hands and elbows above.)

## Round 5 decisions (October 1 evening): art pipeline and approval

- **New pipeline:** image generation (with examples of our art as reference) produces the item concept, the 3D model is built from that image, then the icon is made from the model. Mitchell reviews images in batches.
- **No 3D model is approved yet.** Before approving a model, Mitchell needs proof: in-game, textured renders shown at the same angle as the approved image. Flat untextured previews don't count.
- **Current model problems to fix:** the bows don't match their images at all; the staves can't be judged without textures; gauntlet shapes and textures aren't good enough; Emberforged armour and the uniques don't look as good as their image-gen versions; helmets were missing from the review; the leather body's front strap should be removed; the Ashen Crown needs a real redesign (its approved icon image exists as a starting point).
- **Characters have no thumbs**, which suits the art style. Real hands may come later when characters are improved.
- **Bow draw:** the draw must pull one string from its middle, with the arrow level and pointing at the target.

## Follow-up (October 1, late)

- **The four bows were rebuilt from Codex turnarounds of their approved icons** (`docs/concepts/bow-*.jpg`): each bow's limbs are one smooth piece with the icon's curve and depth, with pointed tips. Mitchell liked the Worn Shortbow and the Hunter's Bow; the Recurve and the Drakebone needed better shape and detail, so they were remade from limb shapes traced off their icons (true recurves: the limbs swing toward the string and the tips curl away).
