- **Five character sheets approved and built** (October 3): the hero, Goblin Grunt, Kobold Slinger, Ember Cultist and Cinder Priest (`D:\dragonbound-archive\codex\concepts2`). Mitchell: every gear piece must fit the new hero, gauntlets included; the Cinder Priest's staff loses the odd block on top for a proper flame head. The hero keeps his body's sizes so all gear still fits, and the starting outfit's extras (collar, strap, pauldron, bracers, boots) come off under the gear that covers them; the sword on his back was left out, since the sword is always in his hand. The Cinder Priest now has his own model, a head taller than his cultists. The redrawn drakeling and Cinderwing sheets were approved the same day and built.
- **Hands are one-piece LEGO hands, never thumbs; arms get elbows** (October 3, rejecting the round 2 characters). Mitchell: "no thumbs look bad on this type of hand, don't ever add them… it has to be all one shape like a lego hand", and the bow draw "needs an elbow obviously". Claude's brief had asked for a thumb, which caused the miss. Now in the game: every humanoid hand (hero, cultists, priest, goblin, Warden, Quartermaster) is one chunky C-clip with a hole and no thumb, and whatever it holds runs through the hole (sword grip, club, staff, bow, the drawn string); gloves and gauntlets of every tier are the same C a size up. Every humanoid arm has an upper arm, forearm and elbow: arms rest a little bent; staffs are carried upright with the forearm forward and stay upright when raised in the cast; a bow is carried plumb and out from the body, since its limbs curve in and would catch the striding leg; the raised sword comes up in line with the forearm; and the bow draw bends the elbow, out to the side, to bring the string hand to the jaw beside the cheek, with nothing passing through the chest (the chunky arm is too short to reach the chin itself). Armour sleeves gained elbow guards so a bent arm never shows a gap. Every character and weapon is checked in every pose for arms or held things cutting into bodies; only the goblin's raised club arm passing behind its big ear remains.

Quartermaster approved. Ember Cultist: no; the hood (third time) "doesnt go up far enough… doesnt come over like a hood properly… back too far". Goblin Grunt: no; the chest strap "isnt attached to anything" and "doesnt go over shoulder", and his shoulder pad floats off the shoulder. - **Characters round 2: hands, hood, staff hold, arms** (October 3, notes in `D:\dragonbound-archive\notes\characters-round2`). Mitchell: "better, but the arms might be clipping to the sides as well on some"; the hand "should all be one piece like a glove", the raised sword wasn't held right, the cultist's staff hold looked weird and its hood was bent back at the top "like it's balding". Fixes: every hand of the hero's kind (hero, cultist, priest, goblin, Warden, Quartermaster) is one solid fist with the thumb across its front, the sword's grip closing in it (raised, the blade comes out of the fist in line with the forearm); the cultist's hood now covers the head in a rounded crown, as in the concept, with a small soft peak tipping forward over the face (a first, taller version read as a gnome's cone); the cult staffs run upright through the fist and stay upright in the raised hand when casting (they used to swing down behind the head); and no arm cuts into a body any more: the arms hang a little out from the sides, the robed and goblin/kobold arms sit against their bodies' sides, and body armour keeps its sides and sleeves on one line (the tunic's own sleeves come off under it). Checked for every character and pose by `fitcheck.py` `arm_clip_all()`. Two overlaps remain by design: the bow's draw arm reaches across the chest to the chin (no elbow to bend), and the goblin's raised club arm passes behind its big ear. (The fist with its thumb and the draw arm across the chest were replaced the same day by the LEGO hands and elbows above.)

## Round 5 decisions (October 1 evening): art pipeline and approval

- **New pipeline:** image generation (with examples of our art as reference) produces the item concept, the 3D model is built from that image, then the icon is made from the model. Mitchell reviews images in batches.
- **No 3D model is approved yet.** Before approving a model, Mitchell needs proof: in-game, textured renders shown at the same angle as the approved image. Flat untextured previews don't count.
- **Current model problems to fix:** the bows don't match their images at all; the staves can't be judged without textures; gauntlet shapes and textures aren't good enough; Emberforged armour and the uniques don't look as good as their image-gen versions; helmets were missing from the review; the leather body's front strap should be removed; the Ashen Crown needs a real redesign (its approved icon image exists as a starting point).
- **Characters have no thumbs**, which suits the art style. Real hands may come later when characters are improved.
- **Bow draw:** the draw must pull one string from its middle, with the arrow level and pointing at the target.

## Follow-up (October 1, late)

- **The four bows were rebuilt from Codex turnarounds of their approved icons** (`docs/concepts/bow-*.jpg`): each bow's limbs are one smooth piece with the icon's curve and depth, with pointed tips. Mitchell liked the Worn Shortbow and the Hunter's Bow; the Recurve and the Drakebone needed better shape and detail, so they were remade from limb shapes traced off their icons (true recurves: the limbs swing toward the string and the tips curl away).
